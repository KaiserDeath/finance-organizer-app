## 0. The single most useful thing I found

**The prototype defines its own scope boundary, and it is exactly the boundary you asked for.**

The prototype contains eight screens — `Hoy`, `Movimientos`, `Análisis`, `Tu dinero`, `Ajustes`,
`Presupuestos`, `Métodos de pago`, `Onboarding` — plus the pay sheet as an overlay. There is **no
Próximos pagos screen and no recurring-templates screen**. Tapping "Pagos recurrentes" in the
prototype's Ajustes fires a toast: *"— fuera del prototipo."*

So for recurring payments the design tells us three things and refuses to tell us the rest:

| The prototype **does** specify | The prototype **does not** specify |
|---|---|
| The Ajustes row: `autorenew` icon, "Pagos recurrentes / Plantillas que se generan solas" | The recurring-templates screen |
| How a due item *appears* on Hoy (the nudge) and in the pay sheet | The Próximos pagos screen |
| The pay sheet's method logic, copy and undo | How occurrences are persisted |
| — | Where you browse next month's occurrences |

This is not a gap to fill by inference. It is the same rule the handoff states for everything else:
*a decision whose premise is missing gets asked about, not half-implemented.* Your instruction and
the design's own boundary agree, which makes this the cheapest constraint in the plan to honour.

**Consequence for the plan:** recurring work is *presentational only, and only on surfaces the
prototype actually draws.* `GetUpcomingPaymentsUseCase` (in-memory projection, never persisted),
`RecurringScreen`, and the Próximos IA stay structurally untouched.

## 2. Defects, ranked by consequence

### 2.1 — The comparison your users rejected is still in the app, and it is broken

**Severity: high.** This is the headline finding.

The user sessions killed the month-over-month delta. I removed it from the hero and deleted
`spendDeltaMinor` / `spendDeltaFraction` / `previousMonthSpentMinor` from the domain. But Análisis
still opens with a **"Ritmo de gasto"** card whose entire job is that same comparison — chart legend
literally reads *"Este mes / Mes pasado."*

Worse, right now it renders **"S/ 0.00 — Sin mes anterior para comparar"** with a flat line, because
the month rolled to August. So the screen's third-largest element is a broken instance of the one
idea testing rejected.

Leaving this is not a cosmetic inconsistency — it means the session result was applied to one
surface and not the app.

> **Resolved in Phase 1.** The card and its month-over-month plumbing are gone from
> `AnalyticsScreen`/`AnalyticsViewModel`. The comparison survives only where it was deliberately
> kept — the monthly report and the exports — under the scope rule now written down in
> `design-decisions.md`.

### 2.2 — Análisis leads with numbers that enable no decision

**Severity: high.** This is audit finding 8, still open.

The screen opens with `Análisis y reportes` → `Tendencias | Reporte` tabs → **Gasto total** and
**Promedio diario** tiles → the broken pace chart → `Gasto por mes`.

The prototype's Análisis opens with *the category you overspent and two exits* — "Ajustar el límite"
and "Ver esos gastos". Block 3's completion criterion was "resolve *you went over in a category,
raise that limit* in two taps from Análisis". **It does not currently resolve in two taps**, because
the overrun card is not the opener. (It has nothing to show today — Presupuestos reads "Todo en
orden" — but the ordering problem is structural, not data-dependent.)

The `Tendencias | Reporte` tabs are also absent from the prototype and add a navigation layer above
content that should lead.

> **Resolved in Phase 1.** The overrun card is the opener, with its two exits; the fixed-expense
> variant changes tone and drops the days-remaining pressure. The "nothing is over" state does not
> collapse and does not merely say so either — `NoOverrunCard` leads with whatever is closest to its
> limit and carries the same two exits, so "two taps to act" holds in both states. The tabs are
> gone: the monthly report is a stacked destination behind the app bar's action. `Gasto total` and
> `Promedio diario` survive, demoted below the actionable card.

### 2.3 — The brand identity is one user toggle away from vanishing

**Severity: high, and architecturally interesting.**

Your "the colors look nothing like the mock" complaint had no code cause. **"Color dinámico" was ON**
in that install (confirmed in the DataStore: `dynamic_color → 12 02 08 01`, then on screen). Material
You was substituting a wallpaper-derived slate for `#4F46E5`. Turning it off snapped the whole
palette to brand.

The deeper issue: the design system commits hard to a brand-first identity — `GlassCard` even
comments that "the tint itself is a fixed brand color" — while the app ships a switch that discards
it. Those two positions cannot both be right. This needs a decision, not a fix (§4, Decision A).

> **Resolved in Phase 4** by Decision A, option 1. No `dynamicColor` path remains anywhere; the
> reasoning sits in `MoneyFlowTheme`'s KDoc. The orphaned `dynamic_color` key that older installs
> still carry is inert, and `SettingsRepositoryTest` pins that it stays inert.

### 2.4 — The one-tap promise is invisible with no explanation

**Severity: medium.** `shortcuts` is empty when onboarding step 4 was skipped and there are under 30
days of history, so `ShortcutsRow` renders nothing at all. The headline feature of block 5 is absent
with no empty state and no way for the user to discover it exists. The prototype never shows this
state, which is precisely why it slipped through.

> **Resolved in Phase 3.** The empty row now says when the shortcuts arrive ("a los 30 días de
> historial") and offers the way in — the *Atajos de un toque* picker, itself added because
> onboarding was the only place shortcuts could ever be chosen. `HeroAndShortcutsTest` covers the
> state on a device, since it is the one that never rendered in development.

### 2.5 — Hero band stops at the status bar

**Severity: low-medium, but it is the remaining visual gap you'll notice.** The prototype's band runs
to the physical top of the screen. Ours starts below the status bar because the shell's `Scaffold`
consumes the top window inset for every destination.

> **Resolved in Phase 3** by Decision B. The shell hands Inicio only the bottom inset
> (`MoneyFlowApp.kt`), and the band applies `statusBarsPadding()` to its own *content*, so the colour
> reaches the top edge while nothing sits under the clock. Once the band scrolls away a scrim of
> exactly the status-bar height keeps the list rows legible underneath.

### 2.6 — "Por pagar S/ 0.00" beside "3 pago(s) vencido(s) · S/ 370.00"

**Severity: medium (correctness of meaning).** `monthPendingMinor` is scoped to the displayed month;
the overdue items are July's. Both numbers are internally defensible and together they read as a bug.

> **Resolved in Phase 3.** The "Por pagar" tile takes `pace.committedRemainingMinor` — what the month
> still owes, including occurrences projected but not yet in the ledger — and falls back to
> `monthPendingMinor` only when there is no pace. The two figures now answer the same question.

### 2.7 — Zero test coverage where all the new code lives

**Severity: medium.** 134 tests pass, but they live in `core/*` and `feature/addedit`.
`feature/dashboard`, `analytics`, `transactions`, `upcoming` and `app` report `NO-SOURCE`.

> **Resolved in Phase 5, then extended.** 166 unit tests pass, and `app`, `dashboard`, `analytics`,
> `budgets` and `upcoming` all have ViewModel coverage. `transactions` still reports `NO-SOURCE` —
> the one feature module without unit tests, and the obvious next piece of work.
>
> The audit did not ask for instrumented coverage, but the same reasoning applies harder there: a
> module with no runner reports zero tests as `BUILD SUCCESSFUL`, which is how `core:ui`'s
> accessibility suite sat dead for months. 49 instrumented tests now cover the migrations, the
> settings file, touch targets, font scale, discreet mode and the pay sheet, and
> `.github/scripts/check_test_counts.py` fails the build when a module with test sources produces
> no results.

## 3. Architecture notes

**Sound, leave alone.** `SpendingPace` is pure, fully derived, and directly testable — the best piece
of domain modelling in the repo. `GetUpcomingPaymentsUseCase`'s decision to project occurrences in
memory and never persist them is correct and well-documented; materialising early would corrupt
balances and analytics. This is exactly what your recurring constraint protects.

**One real smell — ~~open~~ fixed.** `HeroBalanceCard` used to make its own semantic colour decisions
for on-brand surfaces (`onPrimary` for content, `primaryContainer` for secondary text,
`tertiaryContainer` for alerts because red dies on indigo). That knowledge belonged in the design
system, not a feature. It now lives there as `BrandSurfaceColors` / `MaterialTheme.brandSurface`
(`core/designsystem/.../theme/Color.kt`), following the `moneyColors` pattern, and the hero resolves
`container` / `content` / `mutedContent` / `alert` / `track` from it.

The same class of bug turned up again on Presupuestos and was fixed the same way: `NoticeColors`
(warning/danger container pairs) exists because `colorScheme.tertiary` was serving as *text* on a
white surface at 2.15:1. Whenever a component starts choosing a semantic colour, that is the signal
to add a role, not a local `when`.

**Watch.** `DashboardViewModel` combines six sources and derives nudge, insight, pace, budgets,
shortcuts and streak. Still readable, but it is the file most likely to become unmaintainable next.

## 4. Decisions I need from you — *both answered*

These change the plan's shape, so they come first.

> **A: option 1.** The toggle is gone. **B: yes, it was worth it.** The band runs to the top edge.
> Both shipped; see the resolution notes under §2.3 and §2.5.

**Decision A — dynamic colour.** The brand palette and Material You are mutually exclusive identities.
1. **Remove the toggle** (recommended). The redesign is a brand-identity project; the prototype, the
   spec and the design system all assume `#4F46E5`. A switch that silently discards the work you are
   paying for is a liability, and it is why you thought the implementation was wrong.
2. Keep it, but scope it to accents only and pin brand surfaces (hero band, nav) to the brand seed.
   Preserves user choice, costs a real token-layer refactor.
3. Keep as-is and treat brand fidelity as best-effort.

**Decision B — hero band under the status bar.** Full prototype fidelity requires Inicio to opt out
of the shell's top inset and draw edge-to-edge, with the status-bar icons flipped to light over the
band. Roughly a half-day including the light/dark icon handling. Worth it, or accept the current
band?

## 5. The plan

Five phases. Each is independently shippable and ordered by consequence, not size. Phases 1–2 are the
ones that make the app match what you tested.

### Phase 1 — Finish applying the session result (fixes §2.1, §2.2) — **done**

The largest gap between the app and what your users validated.

- Delete the **"Ritmo de gasto"** card from Análisis. It is the rejected comparison, and it is
  currently broken. Remove its month-over-month plumbing from `AnalyticsViewModel` the way it was
  removed from `DashboardData`.
- **Reorder Análisis to lead with the worst-overrun card** and its two exits, per prototype and spec
  §6. Give it a real empty state for "Todo en orden" instead of silently collapsing.
- Demote `Gasto total` / `Promedio diario` below the actionable card, or drop `Promedio diario`
  (finding 8's argument applies to it as squarely as it did to the transaction count).
- Collapse the `Tendencias | Reporte` tabs, or move `Reporte` behind a single action — the prototype
  has no tab layer here.

**Done when:** "you went over in a category, raise that limit" resolves in two taps from Análisis,
and no month-over-month comparison survives anywhere in the app.

> **Met — with the second clause narrowed on purpose.** "Nowhere in the app" turned out to be the
> wrong line and was replaced by *ambient versus requested*: banned on Inicio and Análisis, kept in
> the monthly report and the exports, where a comparison is the entire point of the screen you asked
> for. `DeltaChip`, `CategoryDeltaRow` and the exported PDF still compare, deliberately. See
> `design-decisions.md`.

### Phase 2 — Recurring payments, presentation only (your constraint) — **done**

Bounded by §0. Touch only surfaces the prototype actually draws.

- **Pay sheet** — align to the prototype's spec: amount, due date, suggested method; primary action
  reading "Abrir {app} y registrar" when the method has a linked app and "Registrar el pago" when it
  does not; the explanatory line instead of a dead button; "Ya pagué por fuera" always available as
  secondary; 4 s undo on every path.
- **Recurring marker on rows** — a recurring occurrence should be visually identifiable wherever it
  already appears (Hoy's nudge, Movimientos, the pay sheet). Presentation only.
- **Ajustes row** — match the prototype's `autorenew` icon and "Plantillas que se generan solas".
- **Explicitly out of scope:** `GetUpcomingPaymentsUseCase`, persistence, `RecurringScreen`, and the
  Próximos IA including next-month browsing. If the Upcoming screen looks dated next to the restyled
  sheet, that is a *derived* judgement, not a specified one — raise it, don't silently redesign it.

**Done when:** paying a recurring bill from the nudge opens the right app and returns with the
payment recorded and undoable, and nothing about how recurrences are stored or browsed has changed.

> **Built, and half-verified.** `PaySheet.kt` implements all three method cases with undo on every
> path, and "ya pagué por fuera" persists the chosen method — the behaviour the handoff called
> non-optional, now pinned by `UpcomingViewModelTest` and by
> `paidElsewhere_settlesWithTheSelectedMethod`. The recurring marker is presentation only:
> `TransactionRow` derives it from `recurringId` and the pay sheet marks a projected occurrence
> without claiming a cadence it cannot know. The scope constraint held —
> `GetUpcomingPaymentsUseCase`, persistence and `RecurringScreen` are untouched.
>
> **Still unverified:** the real round trip. A test can prove the intent is fired; it cannot prove
> Yape opens and the user comes back. That needs one pass on a real install.

### Phase 3 — Close the Inicio gaps (fixes §2.4, §2.5, §2.6) — **done**

- **Shortcuts empty state**: when there is nothing to show, say so and offer the way in ("Elige tus
  atajos") rather than rendering nothing. This is the difference between a missing feature and an
  undiscovered one.
- **Band to the top edge** — pending Decision B. *(Answered yes; shipped.)*
- **Reconcile "Por pagar"** with the overdue nudge: either scope both to the same window or relabel
  so two different numbers stop looking like a contradiction.
- Verify the shortcuts grid on device — it has never rendered.

> **All four done**, each noted under its finding above. The device verification became
> `HeroAndShortcutsTest` rather than a one-off look, so the state that never rendered cannot go back
> to never rendering.

### Phase 4 — Design-system consolidation (fixes §2.3, §3) — **done**

- ~~Execute Decision A.~~ Done. No `dynamicColor` path remains; the reasoning is recorded in
  `MoneyFlowTheme`'s KDoc.
- ~~Extract on-brand colour roles into a `heroColors`-style extension beside `moneyColors`.~~ Done,
  as `BrandSurfaceColors` / `brandSurface`. See §3.
- ~~Audit `Presupuestos`, `Métodos de pago` and `Onboarding` against their prototype screens.~~ Done.
  What it found, and what was fixed:
  - **Two breaches of the cross-cutting rules.** Deleting a budget offered no undo, while editing one
    right beside it did — now both do. And `colorScheme.tertiary` was used as *text* on `surface`
    (2.15:1, against a 4.5:1 requirement) for the near-limit warning; the warning is now the
    prototype's filled pill, drawing on the new `NoticeColors` roles.
  - **Touch targets under 48 dp**: the "Abrir" chip on Métodos (now an outlined button), the budget
    editor's chips and segmented buttons, and all of Onboarding's chips (now list rows).
  - **Onboarding was the furthest from the prototype** and was rebuilt: segmented progress and a
    written step counter instead of dots, a back affordance, a brand-surface preview as the promise,
    a keypad instead of a text field with a live per-day figure, 68 dp selectable rows instead of
    chips, a real disabled state on the CTA, and the two exits the summary specifies.
  - **Deliberately not done**, as prototype fidelity rather than defects: the budget editor's icon
    grid and keypad, and the Métodos detail sheet (usage figures, app badges, package id, the
    star-default flow). Métodos still has a FAB the prototype does not, and Presupuestos still has an
    `OverallCard` and an inline "Editar" the prototype folds elsewhere.

### Phase 5 — Tests where the new code lives (fixes §2.7) — **done**

There is **no `MainDispatcherRule` in the repo** — these are the first ViewModel tests, so the
harness comes first (`Dispatchers.setMain`; `junit`, `kotlinx-coroutines-test` and `turbine` are
already wired into every feature module by `AndroidFeatureConventionPlugin`).

> It landed as `core:testing`, which now holds `MainDispatcherRule` *and* the repository fakes, so a
> module does not hand-roll its own. The fakes are stateful with live flows — a no-op stub cannot
> observe the write an undo test depends on, which is exactly how `FakeBudgetRepository` passed
> while proving nothing. There is no mocking library and no `kotlin-reflect`; a test that needs a
> behaviour writes it into a fake. All four ViewModels in the priority order below are covered.

Priority order by risk:
1. `UpcomingViewModel` — that "ya pagué por fuera" **persists the chosen method**. The handoff calls
   this non-optional and it is the one behaviour with no UI affordance proving it worked.
2. `AnalyticsViewModel` — worst overrun, fixed-expense variant, and the Phase 1 removals.
3. `DashboardViewModel` — shortcuts, streak, pace.
4. `MoneyViewModel` — the five row figures.

## 6. Suggested sequencing

Phase 1 first: it is the cheapest, it is the largest correctness gap, and it finishes a decision that
is currently half-applied. Phase 2 next, since it is the constraint you raised. Phase 3 is small and
mostly polish. Phase 4 needs Decision A before it can start. Phase 5 can run in parallel with any of
them and should land before Phase 1's deletions, so the removals are protected.

One commit per phase, per the handoff's one-block-one-PR rule.

> **What actually happened.** The order held, and Phase 5 did land before Phase 1's deletions, which
> is the part that mattered. The one-commit-per-phase rule did not survive contact: a second audit on
> 2026-08-02 raised eighteen further items (D1–D9, U1–U9) that were interleaved with these phases,
> so the history reads by item rather than by phase. The items themselves live in the pull request
> descriptions, not in `docs/` — a gap, and the reason `design-decisions.md` cites U-numbers a reader
> cannot look up here.
